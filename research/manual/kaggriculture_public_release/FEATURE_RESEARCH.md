# Two full feature-family outcome rounds

## Why this is the next research step
The current evidence finally includes action activation for both families. Adding
new decision rules before measuring these would increase the attribution problem.
Round 21 therefore tests the complete collection-service family from screen 19;
round 22 tests the complete maintenance-deadline family from screen 20. Both retain
32 original task descriptors and 12 state summaries. Each adds 12 diagnostic
relative/context fields: **44 task descriptors + 12 summaries per family**.

The tests supplied for each new notebook comprise the 64 user-screened family
checks plus 16 new diagnostic contracts. The two new notebooks have equal scope,
ten evidence views each, and one separately evaluated treatment each. The old
checks overlap between families. No new test has been executed during preparation.

## Hypotheses
**21 — Collection completion.** Changing a collection's score denominator to its
conditional time-to-sale may allocate work toward realizable receipts rather than
nearby goods. Counterhypothesis: it may undervalue profitable multi-collection
routes or distort relative priorities. The new diagnostics quantify within-worker
alternatives, return-work burden, current capacity pressure and conditional rate
gaps. They do not change the implemented actor.

**22 — Maintenance deadlines.** Giving additional priority to survival-critical,
resource-supported tasks with little completion slack may prevent production losses.
Counterhypothesis: the existing policy already handles almost all such cases and
the multiplier may overvalue long trips. New diagnostics quantify competition for
maintenance, exclusive access, resource blocking, relative slack and survival
buffers. They do not remove jobs, change ordinary WATER rules, or alter the actor.

## Fixed design
Use the existing registered b1 block first for both families: seed 2026091217,
seat 0, `livestock_fertilizer`. The seed was proposed before these outcomes and the
returned evidence contains no completed game on it. The original preflight checks
available recorded seed collisions; it cannot certify unrecorded work. Candidate
and control get identical initial conditions and responsive opponents. Compatible
controls are shared, so the maximum new work is three full games across both rounds.

No b2–b4 loop is exposed in the new notebooks. The original broader plan is still
available in the public source, but remains gated on this first evidence. No weights are fit,
no candidate families are combined, and no labels enter feature construction.

## Acceptance and interpretation
The primary local outcome is win/tie/loss, with coin margin and own coins as
separate diagnostics. Preserve the earlier conservative own-cash guard even when
margin changes favorably; report both interpretations. A negative result is retained.
An unchanged new-game action path is nonactivation, not a proof of universal uselessness.
A positive single block is provisional and must not be promoted or converted into a
leaderboard score.

The new relative rankings cover only one family in the current observation. They
are not the exact internal sequence of all policy jobs, since the actor simulates
workers sequentially. Do not label them selected-action probabilities, optimal
allocations, or feature importance. Feature and outcome tables remain separate.

## Broader research remains open
The inherited callback workforce limitation still blocks broad deployment claims.
A public case study is not a submission package. Stronger validation needs both
seats, distinct opponents, fresh groups, and a genuinely untouched final assessment.
Later plausible families include marginal hiring value, joint resource-constrained
service scheduling, production timing, renewal opportunity cost, and calibrated
causal market context. Their future scope should follow the observed outcomes,
not a target feature count.
