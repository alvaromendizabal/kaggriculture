# Feature research: next measurable decision

Status: OPEN RESEARCH. This document is a design decision, not a preregistration,
not a new performance result, and not an assertion that AWS is synchronized.

## Current evidence

The 39-descriptor decision-bottleneck audit has 161 saved-observation parity checks,
28 varying columns and 11 constants. It does not establish incremental predictive
value relative to the older feature bank. The original staffing pilot is halted
at seven accepted games after its eighth attempt exceeded the 500 ms latency gate.
Three complete pairs changed match score by zero. Do not count the missing pair
as a win, discard it from an aggregate claim, or retry it until it happens to pass.

## Prioritized hypothesis

Worker access to profitable tasks and the value of the next alternative action
are more actionable in the observed state slice than more storage-capacity
sensitivity features. All six tested storage-utility sensitivity columns were
zero in these saved states. That is a local sample finding, not a global rejection
of storage features. Useful worker-removal values must be translated into
current-action/option features and tested against equal staffing and market rules.

A state-level value alone does not specify a better action. In particular, route
menus terminate with a DROP at shed access; do not assume that their end positions
provide a new signal without checking where the candidate routes actually end.
Replanning after executing only the first action may be the more relevant mechanism.
Compare action commitment, first-action opportunity cost, and alternatives that
preserve other workers' access before introducing a learned ranking model.

## Required experiment sequence

1. Complete full-callback acceptance of the exact faster assignment implementation.
   Preserve frozen Study 7 source and outcomes. Save rejected payloads and measured
   maxima before gating. Report actual wall time; do not substitute a synthetic clock.
2. Inventory prior development and CI seeds before choosing fresh groups. Use at
   least two meaningfully distinct, source-identified opponents and both seats.
   Verify each opponent changes the relevant task/market opportunity distribution.
3. Define one family intervention and an equal-rule control. Require nonzero
   action divergence confined to its registered window. Otherwise stop for nonactivation.
4. Run one game per durable checkpoint with a hard bounded pilot. Confirm S3
   readback/version/hash before advancing. Failed acceptance is recorded, not erased.
5. Compare paired match score, coin margin, unsold goods, ineffective actions and
   runtime by seed/opponent block. No pooling individual turns as independent samples.
   Any learned thresholds or weights use training episode groups only.
6. Retain a family only with controlled contribution and stable grouped evidence.
   Coverage, feature counts, green CI and prettier plots are not competitive gains.

## Benchmark and completion gate

The user explicitly requires continued feature engineering toward beating the top
score. Do not declare this objective satisfied without an actual comparable,
source-verifiable competition result. Final optimization is not authorized by
candidate counts or by local wins against a single related reference. Training a
small challenger for a feature ablation is research, not declaration of completion.

The Kaggle leaderboard/evaluation pages were checked on 2026-09-11 and did not
expose a readable numeric top rating to this session. Keep the current top rating
unverified; never convert a local match fraction into a leaderboard rating.
The objective is serious competition-level improvement without guaranteeing an
outcome that the evidence does not support.

Primary conceptual reference: Aziz et al., *Multi-Robot Task Allocation --
Complexity and Approximation*, AAMAS 2021, arXiv:2103.12370. This supports studying
explicit coupled allocation constraints; its formal problem is not the Kaggriculture
simulator and it does not establish any game-specific feature-performance claim.
